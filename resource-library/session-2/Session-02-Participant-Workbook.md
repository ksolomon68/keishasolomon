# Session 2: Safe Harbor policy sprint

AI Red-Teaming, Governance, Ethics & Privacy

## Your outcome

Use three realistic scenarios to draft a one-page organizational AI policy, then test whether a colleague can follow it.

### Before you begin

Bring your Session 1 use case and your organization's existing AI/data rules, if available. Use descriptions of data categories rather than actual private records.

### Working boundaries

Use fictional or explicitly approved examples. Do not paste private customer, employee, health or financial records into public AI tools. Follow your organization's tool and data rules. Treat outputs as drafts; a person verifies facts and approves external actions.

### What to keep

Keep a copy of your inputs, outputs, decisions and revisions. Browser-only drafts may not follow you to another device. Upload the completed resource or share a permitted link through your participant dashboard.

### Course coaching

Each cohort participant may request one remote coaching session during the course. Request it inside the platform; your instructor confirms the time. Use the same conversation for time changes.

## Lab guide

Follow your instructor or work through these steps using approved tools. A document or manual simulation is acceptable when a connected tool is unavailable; label its status honestly.

### Step 1

List the tools, tasks, and data categories the policy should cover. Separate what the assistant may draft from what it may send, publish, or change.

### Step 2

For any connected tool, name the account owner, minimum required access, approval point, and way to revoke access. Write allowed, prohibited, and ask-first examples.

### Step 3

Test the draft against a routine task, a sensitive-data request, and an uncertain case. A reusable playbook does not grant permission to act.

### Worked example — fictional / illustrative

Fictional example: Northstar permits brainstorming with invented examples in approved tools, prohibits uploading customer records to public tools, and requires the operations lead to review exceptions. This is an exercise, not legal advice or organizational approval.
